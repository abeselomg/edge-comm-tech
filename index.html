Six homepage directions

Six genuinely different page architectures on one blue-black palette family, each with its own accent and typeface. Same Edge content throughout, so what differs is structure and voice rather than copy. Open any one, then use the bar at the bottom of the screen to flip between them.

01

Split

Fixed left half carries identity, statement and proof. The right half scrolls the catalogue.

Navigation and the pitch are never off-screen. No top bar, no footer.

Manrope

 02

Console

A dashboard grid. Tiles of different span rather than stacked full-width sections.

Reads like the operations console Edge's clients already live in. Scanning order is set by weight.

IBM Plex Sans and Mono

 03

Document

A capability statement issued like a drawing set: masthead, revision block, numbered clauses, dense tables.

The only light direction, and the only one where the page is a deliverable rather than a pitch. Blue-black is the ink here, not the ground.

Newsreader, Inter, Plex Mono

 04

Poster

Near-black and sparse. One statement owns the first screen, then one domain per full-height frame.

A confidence play. Very little on screen at any moment, everything at display size.

Archivo

 05

Topology

The homepage is a diagram: a core, six domain nodes, four sector endpoints, drawn to scale.

Closest to how an integrator explains itself on a whiteboard. The written content hangs off the map.

Space Grotesk

 06

Stack

Organised by layer of infrastructure, not by marketing section. Facility at the base, services at the top.

Reads bottom-up the way a build is actually delivered, with a depth gutter down the left.

Chivo

Before this goes to the client, two things to know.

  • Figures marked with a small square are placeholders. Edge has not confirmed years operating, headcount, project counts or contact details, so none of those numbers are real.
  • The headline, “Designed, commissioned, supported.”, is a stand-in written to hold the space. It is the largest text in every direction and needs Edge’s own line before this is shown as finished work.
